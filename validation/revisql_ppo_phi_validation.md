# ReViSQL / Tinker — validation of φ in RLVR/PPO

**Status: September 2026.** This note extends the earlier Tinker validation of `φ_jump` to a real RLVR/PPO workload based on ReViSQL. It records a negative result and should be read separately from Tests 5–7: those tests established near-instantaneous detection under their own training regimes; the experiment below asks whether the same loss-only signal transfers to signed PPO loss and whether it predicts future policy instability.

## Question

The original loss-only signal is

```text
φ_jump(t) = 0.8 φ_jump(t-1) + 0.2 max(0, ΔL(t))
```

where `L` is the observed training loss. The question was whether `φ_jump`, used passively, can provide useful warning of deterioration during ReViSQL RLVR/PPO training on Qwen3-8B through Tinker.

No actuator was connected to φ in this experiment: φ did not modify learning rate, LoRA rank, sampling, rewards, or any other training parameter.

## Experimental setup

- Model: `Qwen/Qwen3-8B`
- Workload: ReViSQL RLVR / text-to-SQL
- Training backend: Tinker
- Loss: PPO
- LoRA rank: 16
- Learning rate: `1e-4`
- Group size: 2
- Batch size: 2
- Passive run: 50 training steps (100 examples)
- Databases available for the run: `law_episode`, `works_cycles`
- VeriEQL and evidence-supervision interventions disabled for this diagnostic run

Tinker reports `loss:sum`. Because action-token counts vary strongly between batches, the signal was evaluated on

```text
loss_per_token = loss:sum / action_tokens
```

rather than raw `loss:sum`.

The comparison signals were computed on the same `loss_per_token` stream:

- `φ_jump = EMA_0.8(max(0, Δloss_per_token))`
- `abs_delta_loss = |Δloss_per_token|`
- rolling standard deviation
- rolling MAD
- causal/expanding loss z-score
- PPO-KL as an internal PPO reference metric

Future horizons `t+1`, `t+2`, and `t+5` were inspected. Threshold calibration used the first 60% of the trajectory and evaluation the final 40%, chronologically, to avoid calibrating thresholds on future observations.

## Main result: the original φ does not transfer as a reliable PPO predictor

The 50-step trajectory does **not** support the claim that the original one-sided `φ_jump` reliably anticipates PPO instability in this ReViSQL setting.

The strongest φ alarms occurred early in the run (notably around steps 11, 15, and 16). Important PPO-KL excursions occurred elsewhere, including late in the evaluation region, without corresponding φ alarms.

A representative late sequence was:

| step | φ_jump | PPO-KL |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 43 | 0.01649 | 0.62240 |
| 44 | 0.01319 | 1.25181 |
| 45 | 0.01134 | 1.05359 |
| 46 | 0.00907 | 0.70782 |
| 47 | 0.00726 | 0.70730 |
| 48 | 0.00581 | 1.11044 |

Thus φ was decreasing through a region containing several large KL excursions. Recalibrating the threshold on this run does not remove the structural failure.

This result is consistent with the earlier repository conclusion that `φ_jump` should not be described as a demonstrated predictor. It goes further: in this PPO workload it does not provide convincing predictive warning of future KL excursions.

## Why the one-sided definition is structurally problematic in PPO

PPO loss is signed. Its sign does not have the same interpretation as a conventional positive supervised cross-entropy loss, because the surrogate objective depends on signed advantages and clipping.

The original φ only retains upward changes:

```text
max(0, Δloss)
```

and therefore discards large negative loss movements.

Step 48 illustrates the problem:

```text
loss_per_token  ≈ -0.07561
|Δloss|         ≈  0.07561
φ_jump          ≈  0.00581
PPO-KL          ≈  1.11044
```

The large negative loss movement is almost invisible to the one-sided φ even though the same step has high KL. This is evidence of blindness to one direction of the PPO loss dynamics. It is **not**, by itself, evidence that the loss movement predicted the simultaneous KL value; contemporaneous detection (`t+0`) and prediction (`t+h`, h>0) must remain distinct.

## Symmetric follow-up: φ_abs

The obvious post-hoc hypothesis generated by the failure is a symmetric signal:

```text
φ_abs(t) = 0.8 φ_abs(t-1) + 0.2 |ΔL(t)|
```

This can be evaluated offline from the same trajectory, so no additional Tinker training is required.

The 50-step reanalysis does **not** show a convincing recovery of predictive performance. On the chronological evaluation segment, exploratory ROC-AUC values for predicting future KL-spike labels were:

| horizon | φ_jump | φ_abs | rolling STD(5) | |Δloss| | |z-score| |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| t+1 | 0.458 | 0.375 | 0.333 | 0.000 | 0.146 |
| t+2 | 0.578 | 0.533 | 0.411 | 0.333 | 0.222 |
| t+5 | 0.556 | 0.500 | 0.486 | 0.125 | 0.444 |

Values near 0.5 are chance-level rankings. Values below 0.5 indicate that the assumed higher-score = higher-future-risk orientation does not rank the observed events correctly. These numbers are **exploratory only**: the evaluation segment is small and contains few KL-spike events, so they are not precision estimates of population performance.

Using a φ_abs threshold calibrated only on the first 60% (90th percentile, approximately 0.054 in this trajectory), the final 40% produced no φ_abs alarms while containing three KL-spike events under the corresponding calibration rule: 0 true positives and 3 false negatives (recall 0% for this small evaluation segment).

The same general problem affects the simple loss-domain baselines: none establishes reliable future-KL prediction here.

## Reward prediction

The symmetric signal also failed to show a stable advantage for predicting future reward deterioration. Exploratory AUC values for φ_abs were approximately:

| horizon | φ_abs AUC |
|---|---:|
| t+1 | 0.466 |
| t+2 | 0.292 |
| t+5 | 0.600 |

The apparent t+5 value is based on too few evaluation points/events to support a positive claim.

## Verdict

This experiment falsifies the following **setting-specific** claim:

> `EMA(max(0, Δloss))` is a generally transferable predictor of training stress when applied directly to signed PPO loss in ReViSQL/RLVR.

The natural symmetric repair,

> `EMA(|Δloss|)`,

is also **not supported as a useful predictor by this 50-step run** and does not establish an advantage over simple local loss statistics.

This does **not** erase the earlier Tests 5–7 result: `φ_jump` remains an empirically observed near-instantaneous detector in those specific Tinker experiments. Detection in one regime and prediction in a different PPO regime are separate claims.

The broader conclusion supported by this experiment is narrower:

> Loss-only dynamics in this ReViSQL/PPO trajectory were insufficient to provide convincing prediction of future PPO-KL excursions. In particular, making φ symmetric in the sign of the loss change was not enough to solve the problem.

## Interpretation and limits

This is a deliberately negative result, not evidence that no loss-only predictor can ever work in PPO. Important limitations are:

1. one 50-step trajectory;
2. one model and one RLVR workload;
3. only two databases in the diagnostic subset;
4. few KL-spike events in the held-out chronological evaluation region;
5. no multi-seed replication;
6. the PPO loss itself is shaped by group-relative advantages/clipping and is not a generic scalar measure of policy divergence.

Accordingly, the experiment is sufficient to reject a strong transfer claim for the tested φ formulations, but not to establish a universal impossibility result for loss-only monitoring.

## Practical consequence

Do not tune β, windows, or thresholds on these same 50 observations merely to recover a positive result. Any materially changed φ formulation should be declared as a new hypothesis and tested on an independent trajectory.

This result must not be used to tune β, change the sign rule, alter windows, move horizons, or choose a new threshold post hoc. The next scientific step is replication under the already frozen multi-seed protocol and comparison with the other frozen loss-only baselines.
